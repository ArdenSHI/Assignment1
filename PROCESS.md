# PROCESS.md

## What I asked the AI to do

Across the four drafts of this essay, I asked the AI to do four different jobs, and I want to be honest about each.

**Draft 1 (Sept 3).** I asked the AI to write the whole essay from scratch: pick a stance, invent personal anecdotes, draft APA references. I only provided the assignment prompt and one sentence about my background. The AI wrote a confident essay — 867 words, five polished paragraphs — that was also generic. The "VR game" example was invented; the references were accurate but the personal voice was not mine. I rejected this and asked for a second draft where the AI would write nothing until I supplied the substance.

**Draft 2 (Sept 7).** I pasted my own Chinese outline with the four concrete cases (VR brainstorm + blind test between teacher and classmates, project management on the VR game, museum installation, e-commerce builds, the phone-case SKU tool) and asked the AI to (a) polish the English and (b) keep the four cases exactly as I had stated them. The AI's English polish was useful but kept slipping in literary ornaments I had not asked for ("the dangerous one," "the trap called," "the only durable answer for someone who wants to keep making things worth making"). The first polished draft was 1374 words — over the 1000-word limit. I cut, on a case-by-case basis, around 380 words myself.

**Draft 3 (Sept 8, morning).** I told the AI to (a) keep the three-stage structure exactly — finding inspiration, refining inspiration into a structure, producing the finished product, plus a short closing on how the ability is built — (b) keep all four concrete cases intact, (c) strip every emotional adjective and ornamental flourish ("bluntly," "genuinely," "after all," rhetorical dramatic closings), and (d) reformat the references as ACM numbered citations. The AI did the pass; I trimmed the draft by hand to land exactly on 1000 words.

**Draft 4 (Sept 8, later the same day).** I told the AI to (a) shrink the opening to two short paragraphs, (b) expand the three stage-sections with more concrete detail from the same four projects, and (c) keep the total under 1000 words. The AI's draft came in over the limit; I trimmed it back to 1000 words by cutting redundant phrases, not by removing cases or examples.

## What the AI did that I kept

- The clean three-stage scaffolding (finding / refining / producing) which mapped directly onto my outline.
- The closing phrase "the part only you can hold" (now used as the essay's title).
- The English polishing on individual sentences, where it improved clarity without changing what I had said.
- The Manovich reference for the point about defaults encoding what a project is for.

## What the AI did that I rejected

- A numbered ACM reference list. I asked for ACM in draft 3, then switched back to APA in draft 4; the final list is APA.
- Several ornamental phrases across drafts (e.g. "the dangerous one," "the trap called," "the only durable answer, for someone who wants to keep making things worth making"). Cut one by one.
- Any attempt to add new examples, hypothetical cases, or generalising metaphors (photos did not kill painting; synthesisers did not end musicianship). The essay only uses the four cases I supplied.
- A version where the closing turned into an inspirational speech. Asked to end on the practical point instead — fundamentals, daily input, growing the branches to drive the machine.
- An over-long opening that took 200 words to reach the thesis. Asked to compress the setup to a few sentences and let the three stage-sections carry the weight.

## What I did myself

- Wrote the four-case outline (Chinese).
- Verified, by hand, that every cited URL / book still exists and that every claim in the essay corresponds to something I actually did.
- Cut roughly 80 words between drafts 3 and 4 to keep the essay under the 1000-word ceiling.
- Made the final call on what to keep and what to cut, including the choice to switch back to APA from ACM.

## A note on the writing itself

Parts of the essay are word-for-word what I wrote in Chinese; parts are the AI's English polished from what I wrote; a handful of sentences are the AI's tighter version of a thought I had written at three times the length. The cases, the structure, and the judgements in the essay are mine. The English is the AI's.
