# PROCESS.md — How I used AI on this essay

**Tools:** WorkBuddy (AI assistant) — heavily, on both drafts. The first draft argued the question from a directing angle; this version is a full rewrite around a different position that I brought to the table myself: that the skill bridging "describing" and "specifying" is an independent understanding formed by combining programming and design experience, and that my projects (both practical products and art works) decompose into finding inspiration, refining it, and producing the finished thing. The model drafted the prose around that skeleton, and pushed the commits through its GitHub integration.

**What I kept:** The anatomy of the refinement loop — breaking one act of "specifying" into a design judgement followed by its mechanical fix (the easing function, the hue rotation, the per-shape noise seeds). That decomposition is how the loop feels from the inside, and it turned my abstract claim into an argument you can actually check. The lava-lamp example is a fair compression of a real project.

**What I rejected:** The model kept wanting to name the skill "prompt engineering." I rejected that in both drafts, because the name shrinks it: prompt engineering sounds like a technique you can list on a CV, while what I'm describing is a literacy that takes years of programming *and* design to build — naming it after the prompt is like calling directing "giving notes." I also cut a paragraph of borrowed history (photography didn't kill painting, synthesisers didn't kill musicians) that the model produced for the ending. True, but generic — exactly what the assignment warns scores badly — and my argument didn't need someone else's rhyme to land.

**A note on the two drafts:** The first draft was mostly the model's argument with my example attached. This one is built the other way round: my position first, the model as drafter. That difference is, in miniature, the essay's whole point.
